Agriculture: The Foundation of Kogi State

Agriculture has remained one of the most important foundations of the economy and livelihoods of the people of Kogi State. Across generations, farming has provided food, employment, income and raw materials for communities throughout the state.

The agricultural heritage of Kogi State is built around the dedication of smallholder farmers, commercial farmers, cooperatives, women, young people and agricultural entrepreneurs who continue to cultivate the land and contribute to food security.

Today, agriculture continues to occupy an important place in Kogi State's development strategy, with emphasis on food production, farmer support, mechanization, agricultural value chains and food security.

Agricultural Heritage of Kogi State

Kogi State's geographical location and diverse ecological conditions have supported different forms of agricultural production. Farmers cultivate food crops and engage in livestock, fisheries, processing and agricultural trading.

Important crops associated with agricultural production in the state include rice, cassava, maize, yam, sorghum, vegetables and other food and cash crops. Agricultural activities also extend into livestock production, fisheries and agro-processing.

Over the years, the agricultural sector has evolved from largely traditional farming practices toward greater use of improved inputs, mechanization, extension services, processing and organized agricultural programmes.

Ibaji: A Major Agricultural Community

Ibaji Local Government Area occupies an important place in the agricultural story of Kogi State. Farming is central to the livelihoods of many communities in the area, with fertile agricultural lands supporting food production and rural livelihoods.

The agricultural identity of Ibaji is strongly associated with crop production, particularly rice and other food crops. Its agricultural potential makes the area an important part of the state's wider food-security ambitions.

Farmers in Ibaji have continued to contribute to the production of food for households and markets, while agricultural activities also provide employment and income for rural families.

The importance of Ibaji to agriculture has also been recognized in recent state agricultural interventions. During fertilizer distribution in 2024, the Kogi State Government directed that 50 percent of the 25 truckloads of fertilizer be allocated to farmers in Ibaji and Bassa Local Government Areas because of their significant contributions to agriculture in the state.

Odeke: A Community Rooted in Agriculture

Odeke, in Ibaji Local Government Area, forms part of the wider agricultural heritage of the Ibaji people. Agriculture remains closely connected to community life, livelihoods and the preservation of local knowledge.

From one generation to another, farming communities have maintained knowledge of cultivation, seasonal production and the use of available land and natural resources.

The story of agriculture in Kogi State therefore cannot be told without recognizing the contributions of rural communities such as those across Ibaji, including Odeke.

Challenges Facing Agriculture

Despite its enormous agricultural potential, farmers in Kogi State have faced challenges including limited access to farm inputs, mechanization, finance, storage facilities, processing infrastructure, markets and modern agricultural technology.

Addressing these challenges requires cooperation between government, farmers, cooperatives, private investors, development partners and financial institutions.

Leadership and Agricultural Development

Hon. Timothy Ojomah is the Honourable Commissioner for Agriculture and Food Security, Kogi State. According to his official Kogi State Government biography, his roots trace back to Ibaji Local Government Area.

Before his appointment as Commissioner, Hon. Ojomah served in different public-service and community-development roles, including service in Ibaji Local Government and as a board member of the Kogi State Agro-Allied Company.

In 2023, he was appointed Honourable Commissioner for Agriculture, where his responsibilities have included supporting agricultural modernization, mechanization, farmer empowerment and food-security programmes.

Agricultural Development Under Hon. Timothy Ojomah

Under the current agricultural administration, the Kogi State Government has pursued programmes aimed at increasing food production and improving farmers' access to agricultural inputs and services.

Farmer Empowerment

Agricultural programmes have focused on supporting farmers through farm inputs, mechanization and other interventions designed to improve production.

Agricultural Mechanization

The administration has emphasized mechanization as part of efforts to increase farm productivity and reduce some of the difficulties associated with manual farming.

Fertilizer & Inputs

The Ministry has participated in fertilizer and farm-input distribution programmes intended to improve access to essential agricultural inputs.

Food Production

Agricultural interventions have prioritized crops such as rice, maize and cassava as part of the state's food-security strategy.

Youth & Agriculture

Agricultural transformation programmes also emphasize opportunities for young people to participate in modern farming and agribusiness.

Agricultural Investment

The Ministry has also participated in initiatives designed to attract agricultural investment and strengthen value chains across the state.

Food Security and Production

The Kogi State Government has announced agricultural intervention targets covering rice, cassava and maize production. The programme described targets of 5,124 hectares for rice, 1,200 hectares for cassava and 1,200 hectares for maize, with participation across the state's Local Government Areas.

The Ministry has also worked on farmland mapping and intervention planning as part of efforts to increase food production and move Kogi State toward greater food sufficiency.

A Vision for the Future

The future of agriculture in Kogi State depends on empowering farmers, expanding access to finance and modern technology, improving agricultural infrastructure, developing value chains, creating opportunities for women and youth, and building a sustainable food system.

From the farming communities of Ibaji and Odeke to agricultural communities across the entire state, the goal is to build an agricultural sector that creates jobs, improves livelihoods, strengthens food security and contributes to the economic development of Kogi State.
